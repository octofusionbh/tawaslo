Brand
Guidelines

How Orderlo looks, sounds and holds itself together — the logo, the colours, the type and the tone.

Version 1.0 · An Octo Fusion product · Manama, Bahrain
01 — The idea

An open door, in one letter.

Orderlo runs the front-of-house admin for restaurants, cafés, shops and services — orders, pickups, reservations and loyalty, over WhatsApp — so their teams can look after the room instead of the phone.

The mark is an open "O": a ring with a gap, like a table set and ready, or a door left open for a guest. It reads as hospitality and as the first letter of the name at the same time. Calm, confident, welcoming — never loud.

02 — Logo

The logo

The primary logo is the aperture "O" set as the first letter of the wordmark. It is monochrome by design — ink on light backgrounds, white on dark — so it never competes with a photograph or a colour.

The mark alone

Clear space & minimum size

Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of the "O". Don't let type, edges or other marks crowd it. Minimum sizes: logo 90px wide on screen, mark 20px.

03 — Don'ts

Keep it honest

Don't colour the "O" separately
Don't slant, stretch or distort
Don't swap the typeface
Don't place ink logo on low-contrast colour

05 — Typography

Type

Headlines: a serif — Georgia as the safe system default, or a refined serif like Fraunces / Canela when licensed. Body: Inter, or the system sans. The serif gives Orderlo its warmth and confidence; the sans keeps it clear.

Take the order.
Hold the table.
Body copy is set in Inter — plain, warm and specific. Short sentences. Real words. Nothing inflated.

06 — Voice & tone

How Orderlo sounds

Warm, plain-spoken and specific — like a good operator who respects your time. We talk about the room, the order, the regular. We don't do buzzwords or hype.

We do
  • "You opened a café, not a call centre."
  • Short sentences. Real venue words.
  • Speak to the owner, not the industry.
  • Arabic & English, equally at home.
We don't
  • "Leverage our synergistic platform."
  • Exclamation marks and emoji stacks.
  • Vague promises we can't show.
  • Talk down to small businesses.
